# Change request CD-22: SLA timer on tickets

- **Ticket:** CD-22 "SLA timer on tickets" (Epic E5 Notifications & SLA, change request,
  5 pts)
- **Raised:** Sprint 2 (Weeks 4-5), November 2026
- **Status:** accepted, implemented on `feature/CD-22-sla-timer`

## Client message

> "We need to see which tickets are about to miss their response time, before customers
> complain."

## Scope decision

In scope (this change):

- First response and resolution targets per priority, in one place in code
  ([`../sla.md`](../sla.md#policy)).
- Due dates stored on each new ticket at creation, so a later policy change does not move
  them.
- An SLA state per ticket (On track, Due soon at 75% of the time, Breached, Met) for agents
  and admins, on the list and the detail page, with the due time.
- A list filter for Breached and Due soon that works with the existing filters, search,
  sorting and paging, stored in the URL.
- SLA data is internal: customers never receive it.

Decisions (agreed for this change, each can be a later ticket):

- **Calendar hours, not business hours.** Nights and weekends count. Business hours need a
  clinic calendar (opening hours, holidays) that does not exist yet.
- **Only new tickets.** Tickets created before CD-22 show no SLA. No backfill: their
  priority at creation and the policy then are not known for certain.
- **No pause on any status.** `waiting` does not stop the clock. A pause rule needs the
  client to say whose waiting counts (customer, supplier) and is easy to misuse.
- **No email on breach.** The client asked to _see_ the tickets. An alert can reuse the
  CD-21 mailer later.
- Targets in code, not env vars: one reviewed source, tested. They can move to env vars
  with a startup check if the clinic needs different values per environment.

Out of scope: SLA reports or metrics (CD-23), changing a ticket's priority, per-customer or
per-category targets.
